Research interests

Statistical methods that connect uncertainty, computation, and decisions.

My research connects Bayesian statistics, statistical learning and optimization, and biostatistics. Across these areas, I aim to preserve the essential structure of a problem while keeping inference transparent and computation practical.

Bayesian
Statistics

Probability modeling and uncertainty

How can we model probabilities flexibly while keeping uncertainty easy to compute and interpret?

Method

I study kernel-based probability models that borrow evidence across nearby inputs. Beta and Dirichlet conjugate updates provide pointwise posterior summaries for binomial and categorical responses, with scalable approximations for larger datasets.

Learning for constrained decisions

How can we make good decisions when evaluations are expensive and constraints matter?

Method

I combine statistical surrogate modeling with numerical optimization. Bayesian optimization within an exact penalty framework brings objectives and constraints into one sequential search, including problems with equality constraints and infeasible starting points.

Biostatistics

Adaptive learning in clinical studies

How can a clinical trial learn as it progresses while balancing safety and treatment benefit?

Method

I develop Bayesian methods for adaptive clinical trials and dose optimization. This includes borrowing information across doses or indications, incorporating delayed outcomes, and translating updated toxicity and efficacy estimates into interim decisions.
